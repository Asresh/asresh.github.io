01. RTL Design

Clean, synthesizable SystemVerilog — one self-contained, self-checking digital block a day. Every design ships with a golden-reference scoreboard and a Makefile flow for Icarus, Verilator, VCS and Questa, from CDC-safe FIFOs and IEEE-754 arithmetic all the way up to GPU/SIMT microarchitecture.

Featured Build

Output-Stationary Systolic-Array GEMM

An N×N mesh of signed multiply-accumulate PEs in a TPU-tile, output-stationary dataflow. Operands stream in nearest-neighbour — activations from the west, weights from the south — with an automatic diagonal-skew scheduler that lines them up so every product lands in the right accumulator. A start/busy/done handshake wraps the whole tile, verified against a longint golden matmul scoreboard.

02. Design Verification

UVM environments that actually sign off a DUT — layered and virtual sequences driving real bus agents, reference-model scoreboards checking every transaction, functional and cross coverage closing the space, and SVA guarding the protocol. Constrained-random for breadth, directed corners for the mean bugs.

03. Hardware–Software Co-Design

Full-stack accelerators. Each build pairs parameterized SystemVerilog RTL with a real bus, working bare-metal C firmware that configures and drives it, and a differential testbench that proves the hardware bit-exact against a C golden model — then measures the cycle-count speedup over a scalar baseline. Continuous integration runs the whole thing under Icarus on every push.

Featured Accelerator

INT8 Systolic GEMM Accelerator

An 8×8 INT8 output-stationary MAC array with K-accumulate tiling, driven over a Wishbone B4 control plane by a tiled-GEMM C driver. Up to 64 MACs a cycle for a measured 49.35× speedup over the scalar baseline — and across 273 jobs and 17,152 checked outputs, the differential testbench found zero mismatches.

04. AI Hardware Acceleration

Silicon for machine learning. The through-line across everything above: quantized matmul, structured sparsity, and the GPU/SIMT dataflow primitives that ML kernels are actually built on — designed as RTL, signed off in UVM, and co-designed with the C that drives them.

The Flagship

Quantized Matmul, End to End

The matmul engine behind quantized inference, taken from RTL to running system. It appears as an output-stationary systolic array in RTL, gets its reduction lane signed off in a UVM tensor-core scoreboard, and lands as an 8×8 INT8 GEMM accelerator driven by tiled-GEMM C firmware — 49.35× over scalar, bit-exact.
